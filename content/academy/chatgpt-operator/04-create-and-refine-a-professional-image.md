# CG L04: Create and Refine a Professional Image

## What you will finish

Create a professional profile or campaign image with a clear purpose, composition, style, text rule, and revision process. When you are done you will have one finished image saved at the right shape for where it is going, plus the prompt and the one revision note that got it there.

## Why this matters

Look at the Google Business Profile for most local businesses. The profile photo is a logo squeezed into a circle, or a phone selfie from 2018, or nothing. The Facebook cover is a stock photo of a handshake. People decide whether to call in about three seconds, and this is what they see.

A realtor in Henderson told me she had been meaning to get headshots for two years. A photographer costs money, takes a morning, and she never scheduled it. Meanwhile her profile picture was cropped out of a group shot at a closing.

ChatGPT can make images now, and it can work from a photo you upload. That does not replace a photographer for everything. It does get you a clean, honest profile image and a usable campaign graphic this afternoon.

The catch is that most people prompt it like a wish. "Make me a professional headshot." What comes back has a strange hand, a garbled sign in the background, and a jawline that is not yours. This lesson is the process that gets you to something you would actually post. It opens the Intermediate level, where the project and prompt skills start turning into assets.

## The lesson

### 1. Define intended use

Before you type anything, answer one question: where is this image going?

A profile picture is a small circle. Google, Facebook, LinkedIn, your email signature. Face centered, head and shoulders, plain background, because at forty pixels wide nothing else survives.

A campaign image is a rectangle. A Facebook ad, a post, a website banner. It needs room for a headline that you will add later, so the subject sits to one side and the other side stays open.

A cover image is wide and short, and on some platforms the middle gets covered by your profile circle.

The use decides the crop, the shape, and how much empty space you need. Say it in the prompt: "square profile picture for Google Business Profile" or "landscape marketing image for a Facebook post about spring cleanups, with the left third open for a headline."

What good looks like: you can name the platform, the shape, and what gets placed on top of the image before you generate it.

The mistake is making a beautiful image and then discovering it is the wrong shape. You crop it and lose the subject. Decide the use first.

Example: a pressure washer in Hallsville needs a post image. He asks for landscape, subject on the right, a clean wet driveway in the foreground, left side open.

### 2. Describe subject and composition

Now build the prompt. The exact working prompt below has the slots. Fill them like you are describing the shot to a photographer who has never met you.

Subject. Who or what, doing what, where. "A woman in her forties in a navy blazer, standing in front of a brick storefront, relaxed, looking at the camera." For a real person, upload a reference photo first. Click the plus button in the message box, add the photo, and say in the prompt that a reference is supplied. The constraint about preserving natural facial proportions matters here. You want it to look like you, not like a cousin.

Composition. Crop, placement, negative space. "Head and shoulders, centered, plain background" for a profile. "Subject right third, left two-thirds open, soft background" for a campaign image.

Style. Photorealistic, editorial, or illustration. Pick one. For a profile picture, photorealistic. For a campaign image, an editorial photo or a clean illustration both work.

Lighting and mood. "Soft natural light, late afternoon, calm." Colors. "Warm off-white background, navy and brass accents." Keep the palette to two or three colors so it matches your site.

Constraints. Keep the ones in the prompt. No invented logos, because it will make one up and it will be wrong. No watermark. No text unless quoted verbatim, because AI images misspell things, and even quoted text needs checking letter by letter. My rule: if the image needs words, add them later in Canva or Figma.

To generate, paste the prompt into the chat. If your tools menu shows Create image, you can pick it, but describing the image usually starts it on its own. Ask for one polished direction, not four. Four thumbnails means you pick without looking closely at any of them.

The mistake is describing the feeling instead of the picture. "Professional and trustworthy" is not a subject. "Standing beside her work truck with the door lettering out of frame" is.

Example: a gym in Tyler asks for an editorial photo of an empty squat rack in morning light, cream wall, one cobalt band on the floor, nothing else. It gets a clean campaign image with room for a headline.

### 3. Revise one variable at a time

The image comes back. Before you react to whether you like it, inspect it.

Face. Does it look like you, or like a smoothed-over version? Eyes even, teeth normal, ears matching. Hands. Count fingers. Check where they touch things. Text. Any sign, badge, or lettering in the background will be garbled. Edges. Hair against the background, glasses frames, where the shoulder meets the blazer. Brand fit. Do the colors and setting match your website and your other photos, or does it look like a different company?

Write down what is wrong. Then change one thing. "Keep everything the same. Remove the sign in the background and make the wall plain warm white." Wait. Inspect again. Then the next thing.

If you ask for three changes at once, it re-imagines the whole image and you lose what was working. One variable, one pass.

Download the final with the download control on the image. Name the file for its use: renee-profile-google-2026-09.png. If you need an exact pixel size, resize it in whatever tool you already use.

One honest sentence and we move on. Do not present a generated picture as a real photo of your team, your shop, or your finished work. A generated headshot built from your own photo is fine. A generated "before and after" of a driveway you never cleaned is not.

What good looks like: you could put the image next to your real photos and nothing about it would make a customer pause.

The mistake is settling for the first image because it is close. Close is what people notice. Fix the hand.

Example: a church in Marshall makes an illustration for a fall event post. First image has a garbled banner. Second pass removes the banner. Third pass warms the palette. Done in three prompts.

## Worked example

Cherokee Trace Realty in Henderson is a fictional example. Renee is the broker. She needs one profile picture that works on Google Business Profile, Facebook, and her email signature. She has a decent phone photo of herself in front of her office, but the lighting is harsh and there is a trash can in the shot.

She opens a chat in her project named "Profile picture," uploads the phone photo with the plus button, and fills in the exact working prompt. Purpose: square profile picture for Google Business Profile and Facebook. Subject: the woman in the reference photo, same face, same hair, navy blazer, relaxed, looking at the camera. Composition: head and shoulders, centered, plain background, face fills about half the frame. Style: photorealistic. Lighting and mood: soft natural window light, calm, approachable. Colors: warm off-white background, navy blazer, no other strong colors. Constraints as written. One polished direction.

Example output:

```text
Here is one polished direction based on your reference photo.
The image shows a head-and-shoulders portrait, centered, warm off-white background, soft window light from the left, navy blazer.
I preserved the facial structure and hair from your reference and removed the background elements.
No text, logos, or watermark were added.
Let me know if you would like a different crop, background tone, or lighting.
```

She inspects it. Face: close, but the smile shows more teeth than her real smile does. Hands: not in frame, good. Text: none. Edges: hair against the background is clean. Brand fit: the navy matches her site.

Review against the pass standard. Fits the intended placement: square, centered, reads at small size. Avoids visible artifacts: one, the smile. Follows every identity and text constraint: yes.

The one targeted revision: "Keep everything exactly the same. Match the mouth and smile to the reference photo more closely, lips slightly closed." The second image comes back with her real smile. She downloads it, names it renee-profile-2026-09.png, and uploads it to Google and Facebook the same afternoon.

## Exact working prompt

```text
Create a professional [profile picture or marketing image] for [purpose]. Subject: [describe]. Composition: [crop, placement, negative space]. Style: [photorealistic, editorial, illustration]. Lighting and mood: [describe]. Colors: [palette]. Constraints: preserve natural facial proportions if a reference is supplied, no invented logos, no watermark, and no text unless quoted verbatim. Produce one polished direction.
```

## Guided practice

1. Write down where the image is going and what shape that place needs.
2. If it is a picture of you, pick one clear reference photo and upload it in a new project chat.
3. Fill every slot in the exact working prompt and generate one direction.
4. Inspect face, hands, text, edges, and brand fit, and write down what is wrong.
5. Make one targeted revision, inspect again, download, and name the file for its use.

## Assignment and evidence

Generate one image, inspect the face, hands, text, edges, and brand fit, then make one targeted revision.

Save the source material, the prompt, the first result, the final result, and your review notes.

## Pass standard

The image fits its intended placement, avoids visible artifacts, and follows every identity and text constraint.

## Common mistakes

- Generating before deciding where the image goes. Fix: name the platform and the shape first.
- Describing a feeling instead of a picture. Fix: subject, setting, crop, placement, light, colors.
- Asking for text inside the image. Fix: add words later in Canva or Figma, or quote them exactly and check every letter.
- Requesting three fixes at once. Fix: one variable per pass, inspect between passes.
- Posting a generated image as a real photo of your work. Fix: use generated images for portraits and graphics, not for proof.
