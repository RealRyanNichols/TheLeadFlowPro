# AD L07: Launch a Controlled Test

## What you will finish

Start small with approved creative, fixed budgets, exclusions, and a documented review date. When you are done you will have a completed launch checklist, a dated folder of screenshots of every live setting, a signed brief, and a review date on the calendar before a dollar is spent.

## Why this matters

Meta's editor is built to say yes on your behalf. When you duplicate an ad, the dialog comes pre-checked to add a video and to turn on SMS verification on your form. It suggests "related media" and quietly pulls the picture ads you paused in beside your winning video. Text improvements move a line of your primary text into the headline slot. And in a leads campaign with a single ad set, the audience editor disappears entirely. There is no targeting field to fill in, whatever you named the ad set.

Then there is the login. For a while my browser was signed into Facebook as someone who had helped set things up. The business assets were mine. The session in front of them was not. That is why the two-factor prompt never came to my phone. If I had needed to pause something from the road, I could not have.

A checklist with screenshots is how you know what actually went live, as opposed to what you meant to publish. Bring everything: the brief, the budget sheet, the three ad briefs, the nine assets, the page, and the measurement map. This lesson is where they meet the platform.

## The lesson

### 1. Complete preflight

Go down the list. Do not skip a line because it seems obvious. The obvious ones are the ones that bite.

Access. You own the business portfolio, the page, the ad account, and the pixel. Not an agency, not a former helper. Two-factor goes to your phone. Log out and log back in as yourself to prove it.

Billing. Your card, on your account. Set the account spending limit equal to the total cap from lesson two. That limit is the one stop rule the platform enforces for you.

Geography and exclusions. The radius and the exclusions from the brief, typed in exactly. Read the estimated audience number and compare it to the number on the brief. If you have a customer list, exclude current customers so you do not pay to reach people who already bought.

Placement. Automatic placements are fine to start. In my account the spend landed on Reels and Feed, no Audience Network, no right column. Look anyway.

Budget and schedule. Budget at the ad set level, the daily cap from the sheet. If you ever want a second ad set, budget has to live here, and Meta locks you out for two hours if you flip it back. A start date and an end date. The end date is the total cap in days.

Creative and claims. The nine labeled assets, uploaded, named to match the tracker. Every enhancement off. Related media declined. Every line of every ad checked against the proof column. If a claim moved in the edit, it goes back or it goes out.

Landing page and events. The message-match audit from lesson five, passed. The form's question order, confirmed. The test click from lesson six, passed today, not last week. Form success firing once. Lead row landing with source fields.

Notifications. The lead alert goes to a phone a person answers, and that person knows it is coming.

Naming and approval. Business, offer, format, area, the same words in Meta, in the file names, and in the lead table. Then the operator signs the brief with today's date. On a one person business that is you, and you still sign it.

Pause procedure. Who can pause, and how, in one tap. The Meta Ads app on your phone, logged in as you, tested once.

If you are launching Google Local Services instead, the preflight adds a license, insurance, and a background check upload, and the budget is set as a weekly lead target. For Search, add negative keywords and set location to people in the area, not people interested in it.

What good looks like: every line has a yes and a screenshot next to it.

The mistake: accepting whatever the editor pre-filled. Meta's defaults are Meta's plan, not yours.

### 2. Limit changes

Freeze it for 72 hours. Every edit resets the learning. In my account, three campaigns splitting one budget never got out of learning, and consolidating them into one campaign at $60 a day is what made the first honest read possible.

One offer, one ad set, three to nine ads. New creative that arrives later goes into its own ad set with its own budget, or into the A/B test tool, so it gets a fair read instead of the nineteen cents my third ad got.

If something is broken on day one, a wrong link, a wrong price, a form pointing at the old offer, fix it, write down what you fixed and when, and restart the 72 hour clock. Broken is the only reason to touch it.

What good looks like: a change log with one entry, the launch, until the review date.

The mistake: a little tweak every morning. A headline here, a budget bump there. Each one feels harmless. Together they mean the campaign never gets 72 clean hours to tell you anything.

Example: a gym in Longview launches on a Monday, bumps the budget Tuesday because "it is working," swaps a headline Wednesday, and by Friday cannot say which of three campaigns it is looking at. It is looking at none of them.

### 3. Record the baseline

Before you publish, screenshot every live setting. Campaign objective. Ad set audience, radius, exclusions, and the estimated audience number. Budget and schedule. Placements. Each ad's primary text, headline, description, and media. The form's questions in order. The URL parameters. The account spending limit. Date-stamp the folder.

Then the day zero log. Spend zero, leads zero, and the review date and time on the calendar, 72 hours out. Write one sentence about what you expect to see, so you can be wrong honestly instead of remembering it differently.

Publish. Watch for a phantom error. I have seen the editor show "Fix error" on a freshly created ad three times, and each time it validated clean and published anyway. Confirm every ad reads Active an hour later, not In review and not Preparing. Do the test click one more time, live.

Then put the phone down. The next time you open Ads Manager is the review date.

What good looks like: a folder named with today's date that a stranger could use to rebuild the campaign exactly.

The mistake: "I will screenshot it later." Later, Meta has already suggested three changes and you cannot remember which ones you accepted.

## Worked example

Rabbit Creek Plumbing in Kilgore is a fictional example. Launch on Meta: one campaign, one ad set, angle 1 and angle 2's three assets each, $35 a day, $500 total cap, Kilgore plus 20 miles, Tyler excluded. Marisol pasted the brief, the budget sheet, the tracker rows for six assets, the form id, the naming scheme, and the pause procedure.

"Create a campaign launch checklist for Meta, Rabbit Creek water heater campaign, Kilgore, October. Cover account access, billing, geography, exclusions, placement, budget, schedule, creative, claims, landing page, events, notifications, naming, approval, and pause procedure."

Example output:

```text
Access: portfolio, page, ad account, pixel owned by Marisol; two-factor to her phone; verified by logout and login.
Billing: her card; account spending limit $500.
Geography: Kilgore pin, 25 mile radius, exclude Tyler. Estimated audience 96,000.
Budget: $35 per day at ad set level. Schedule: Oct 6 to Oct 20.
Creative: six assets uploaded, enhancements off, related media declined. Claims checked against matrix.
Events: test click passed Oct 5. Notifications: office phone, Marisol backup.
Pause: Meta Ads app on Marisol's phone, tested. Review date: Oct 9, 7 PM.
```

Review against the pass standard. Do budget, area, dates, creative, page, tracking, and approval exactly match the brief? Budget, dates, creative, page, tracking, yes. Approval, not yet signed. Area, no. The brief says 20 miles. The ad set says 25, because Meta's default radius filled the field and nobody retyped it. That extra five miles reaches Overton and the west edge of Tyler, which the brief excludes.

The one revision: the radius was retyped to 20 miles, the estimated audience dropped to about 71,000, the screenshot was retaken, and Marisol signed the brief with the date. Then it published, every ad read Active an hour later, and the live test click landed as one row with the right source fields.


## Guided practice

1. Log out of Meta and log back in as yourself, then confirm the portfolio, page, ad account, and pixel are under your name.
2. Set the account spending limit to your total cap from lesson two.
3. Run the exact prompt with your brief, budget sheet, tracker, and form id pasted above it.
4. Go line by line through the checklist and screenshot each live setting into a dated folder.
5. Put the 72 hour review date on the calendar, sign the brief, publish, and confirm every ad reads Active.

## Pass standard

Budget, area, dates, creative, page, tracking, and approval exactly match the brief.

## Common mistakes

- Accepting the duplicate dialog's defaults. Fix: uncheck add a video and SMS verification every time.
- Related media and text improvements left on. Fix: decline, publish, and check again.
- Radius left at Meta's default. Fix: type the brief's number and read the audience estimate.
- No account spending limit. Fix: set it to the total cap before the first ad goes live.
- Editing on day two. Fix: freeze for 72 hours unless something is broken, and log the fix.
