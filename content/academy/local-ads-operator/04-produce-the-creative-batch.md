# AD L04: Produce the Creative Batch

## What you will finish

Turn three angles into a controlled set of video, image, and text variations. When you are done you will have nine labeled files on your phone or drive, a tracker row for each that says what it is testing, and the proof behind every claim attached to the row.

## Why this matters

Creative is the only part of the ad the viewer actually sees. Targeting is a suggestion Meta may or may not take. In my account the still picture ads ran between $4.41 and $4.48 a click. The phone video ran $0.68. Meta charged more than double per thousand views to show the pictures, because people scrolled past them, and less to show the video, because people stopped.

Here is what that video was. Me, walking Longview Mall with a phone, asking owners one question. Other people on camera answering it. A place anyone in town recognizes. It opened with a question about the viewer, not a claim about me. The video that lost had better production: a lake behind me, clean audio, five proof cards fading in on cue. It opened with what I had built. Nobody cared yet.

So this lesson is not "make nine ads." It is nine controlled versions, each one changing one thing, each one labeled so that when the weekly report says a file won, you know exactly which file and why.

Bring the three ad briefs from lesson three. The proof column is your permission slip. No proof, no shot.

## The lesson

### 1. Hold one variable steady

Three angles. Three formats per angle: a phone video, a still image, and a text-forward version. That is nine. Inside each angle the message stays the same and the format changes. Across angles the format stays the same and the message changes. That is the whole design.

Write the hypothesis on every version before you make it. "Angle 2 video with the question opener will get more form opens than the angle 2 still, because the question stops the thumb." If you cannot write the sentence, you do not know what the version is for.

Meta allows one call to action button per ad, so you cannot test that inside an ad anyway. I use Learn more on everything and move on.

One structural thing, and it cost me. When several ads share one budget, Meta picks a favorite in the first hours and starves the rest. One of my ads got nineteen cents out of $110. It was never tested. If you want a fair read on new creative, it goes in its own ad set with its own budget, or into Meta's A/B test tool. Lesson seven covers the setup. Plan for it now.

What good looks like: nine rows, and each row differs from its neighbor in exactly one column.

The mistake: changing the hook, the format, and the length all at once and then "learning" from it.

### 2. Design for the placement

Look at where the money goes. In one two day read, most of my spend landed on Facebook Reels and Instagram Reels, then Feed. No right column, no Audience Network. That means vertical. Shoot 1080 by 1920 on the phone, and crop a square version for Feed later.

Here is the video recipe that worked, and I would start every local owner here. Walk a real place your customers know. The town square, your shop, a job site with the homeowner's OK. Open with a question about them in the first three seconds. Get other people on camera, and get a one line verbal OK from each of them before their face goes in a paid ad. That OK is good footage too. Burn captions in, because the sound is off. Twenty five to thirty five seconds if it is a walk with a payoff. Eight to fifteen if it is you talking to the lens. A talking head does not get the extra rope.

If you show your work on a screen, record it on the phone, not the laptop. Open the page in Safari, scroll to the spot first, record five or six seconds, one motion per clip, a scroll or a tap, not both. Cut each to two seconds.

For the still image: one real photo, one line of text. No stock. Stock reads fake in a town where people know your truck.

For the text: primary text in short lines, the question first. A headline. A description. Meta lets you load up to five headlines and five descriptions for it to rotate. My winning ad had one of each and my losing ad had five of each, so build the winner out, not the loser.

Turn off the creative enhancements. Meta will offer to improve your text, add related media, and adjust your image. Decline all of it, and check again after you duplicate anything, because the boxes come back checked. What runs should be what you wrote.

If you are also on Google Search, the text version becomes the ad: up to fifteen headlines, four descriptions, the matrix hooks with the town name in them.

What good looks like: a vertical video with a question in the first three seconds, a still with one line, and copy that reads like a person talking.

The mistake: a talking head in a nice location. Good production is not an angle.

### 3. Label every version

The file name says everything. Business, offer, angle, format, version. RCP_WH_A2_video_v1.mp4. The ad name in Meta matches the file name. The tracker in the workbook has a row per asset: file name, angle, format, placement, hypothesis, the claim it makes, the proof file for that claim, and status.

Attach the proof. The invoice count. The review screenshot. The ten second clip of the homeowner saying "sure, use it." When someone asks in six months whether that number was real, the answer is in the row.

Nine assets is the assignment. If video scares you, do the three stills and the three text versions first, then shoot one walk. Then two more. The walk is the one that earns its budget.

What good looks like: you can read a file name in Ads Manager and say which angle, which format, and which version without opening anything.

The mistake: final_FINAL2.mp4 and an ad called "New ad copy 3." You will not remember which one won, and Meta will not tell you in words you can use.

## Worked example

Rabbit Creek Plumbing in Kilgore is a fictional example. Marisol brought the three briefs: angle 1, the cold shower this morning; angle 2, nobody called me back; angle 3, the real price. She pasted the briefs, the proof files listed for each, and one constraint: no before and after photos, and no claim about response time she cannot show from the office callback log.

"Create a creative production plan for three approved ad angles. For each, write a 20-second video script, static image brief, primary text, headline, description, exact call to action, file name, and hypothesis. Avoid before-and-after claims unless verified and allowed."

Example output, angle 2:

```text
Video, 20 seconds. 0 to 3: Marisol in the office, phone ringing, to lens: "Who called you back the last time your water heater quit?" 3 to 10: office manager picks up a real call, caption "7 AM to 7 PM, a person." 10 to 16: "We always answer. Within the hour, every time." 16 to 20: "Tell us what it is doing. We will call you back." CTA: Learn more.
Still: office phone in hand, one line: "Who called you back last time?"
Primary text: the question, three short lines, the callback promise, the 7 to 7 limit.
File: RCP_WH_A2_video_v1.mp4. Hypothesis: question opener beats the still on form opens.
```

Review against the pass standard. Does each asset match its angle, placement, approved claim, and version label? Angle, placement, and label, yes. Claim, no. "We always answer" is not in the approved matrix, and Marisol cannot back it at 2 AM. The approved promise is a callback within the hour, 7 to 7.

The one revision: the 10 to 16 line became "A real person calls you back within the hour, 7 to 7. Here is the log," with a two second shot of the callback log on the office screen. The claim now matches the proof file in the tracker row, and the same fix went into the primary text.


## Guided practice

1. Write the nine file names before you make anything, and one hypothesis sentence per file.
2. Run the exact prompt with your three ad briefs and proof list pasted above it.
3. Shoot the first walk video on your phone, vertical, question in the first three seconds, verbal OK from anyone on camera.
4. Make the three stills from real photos with one line of text each.
5. Fill the tracker row for every asset and attach the proof file to each claim.

## Pass standard

Each asset matches its angle, placement, approved claim, and version label.

## Common mistakes

- Opening with who you are. Fix: open with a question about them, in the first three seconds.
- A talking head in a nice location. Fix: a real place people recognize, other people on camera, motion.
- Changing three things per version. Fix: one variable and one hypothesis sentence per file.
- Letting Meta enhance the creative. Fix: decline every enhancement and check again after duplicating.
- Unlabeled files. Fix: business, offer, angle, format, version in the file name and the ad name.
