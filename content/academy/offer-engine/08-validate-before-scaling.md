# OE L08: Validate Before Scaling

## What you will finish

Run a small evidence-gathering launch before spending heavily. When you are done you will have a seven-day validation plan, a filled scorecard from five real conversations or five qualified page visits, and one written decision: continue, revise, or stop.

## Why this matters

An owner in Longview, fictional, put $2,000 into Facebook ads pointed at a page nobody had ever bought from. Twelve leads. Zero sales. He concluded that ads do not work. The ads worked fine. They sent twelve strangers to an offer that had never been said out loud to a human.

That is the cost of skipping this lesson. Not the $2,000. The wrong conclusion, which costs a year.

Everything you built in this course is a set of guesses. Good guesses, built from evidence, but guesses. The buyer brief, the result, the scope, the price, the answers, the page. Seven days and five conversations will tell you more than a month of rewriting.

This is the last lesson. Bring the offer page from lesson seven. This is where you find out.

## The lesson

Two rules before we start. First, permissioned outreach only. People who know you, who asked to hear from you, or who you can reach through a channel they chose. No bought lists, no scraped numbers, no cold texts. If you are unsure whether a contact is fair, they are not. If any of your outreach is by text, have your attorney review your consent language once and move on. Second, owned channels only. Your page, your Google Business Profile, your email list, your phone, your truck. No paid ads this week. Ads come later, in The Local Ads Operator, after you have proof.

### 1. Choose a small audience

Five is the number. Five conversations with people who fit the buyer brief, or five qualified visits to the page from people who fit it. Not fifty. Five you can reach and learn from.

How to do it: go back to the three names under your buyer brief. Add two more from your phone: past buyers, people who asked for a quote and said "keep me posted," a current client who could introduce you to one more. Write down how you will reach each one and why that channel is fair. If you would rather test the page than talk, post it once to your Google Business Profile and your own social page, and send it to your existing list with a plain note. A qualified visit is someone who fits the brief and reached the call to action. Not a cousin being nice.

What good looks like: five names, a channel next to each, a date inside the next seven days, and a permission note. "Referred by Tim at Timberline, he said to call."

The mistake: going wide to feel productive. Twenty strangers teach you less than five right people, and they cost goodwill in a small town.

Example: Reyes Bookkeeping, Kilgore, fictional. Her five: two current clients who fit, one contractor a client offered to introduce, one owner who asked for a quote in March and said to follow up, and one from her church who brought it up himself. All permissioned, all reachable this week, no list needed.

### 2. Define pass and fail signals

Decide what counts before you start, or you will grade the week on how you feel on Friday.

How to do it: write three or four signals with numbers. Problem recognition: how many of the five describe the problem in their own words before you lead them? Fit: how many say "that's me" or name someone who is? Price: how many react to the number with a question instead of a laugh? Action: how many take the next step, a walk-through, a sample, a booking? For a first test I set the line at three of five recognize the problem, two of five take the next step, and nobody rejects the price without giving a reason. Below that, revise. If nobody recognizes the problem, stop and go back to lesson one. That is not failure. That is a week well spent.

What good looks like: the thresholds written at the top of the workbook Validation scorecard before the first call, and the decision rule under them. Continue if all pass. Revise if one fails. Stop and rethink the buyer if problem recognition fails.

The mistake: measuring compliments. "Sounds great" is not a signal. A calendar invite is. A question about the contract is. "Can you do my brother's place too" is.

For an outside read on the business, the Business Growth Diagnostic at /diagnostic is free and takes a few minutes.

Example: Sabine Strength, Longview, fictional. Pass signals for the six-week intro: three of five say they have quit a gym before and why, two of five book the free watch-a-session visit, at least one asks what happens after week six. They tested with five people from their member referral list. Four booked. The one who did not said the mornings were wrong. That became a second time slot, not a new offer.

### 3. Record questions and decisions

The conversations are the product of this week. Write them down like evidence, because they are.

How to do it: the same five questions to everyone, in the same order. When did this problem last cost you something? What did you try? What would have to be true for you to fix it this month? Here is what I do and what it costs, what is your first question? What would make this an easy yes? Then record, the same day, on the scorecard: their exact words, the objection they raised, whether it was already in your bank, what they did next, and what you changed.

At the end of day seven, write the decision in one line and the reason in one more. Continue: open the founding offer to the first three. Revise: name the one thing that changes, and run five more. Stop: go back to the lesson where the signal failed.

What good looks like: five rows, exact words, a next action for each, and a dated decision at the bottom. Plus a list of the new objections, because they go straight into your bank and onto your page.

The mistake: not writing it down because you will remember. You will remember the compliment. You will forget the question that would have changed the offer.

Now run the prompt with your offer pasted in. It returns a day-by-day plan, the questions, thresholds, what to record, and the decision at the end. Change the thresholds to your own numbers. Then start on day one, not next week.

When the week is over, look at what you are holding. A buyer brief, a result statement, a scope card, a proof inventory, a price floor, an objection bank, an offer page, and a scorecard with real words on it. That is an offer. Most businesses in this county have never had one written down. The next course is The Lead Capture System, where you build the free thing that brings the right people to this page and the form that puts them into a record you own. The Follow-Up Engine comes after that, for the people who raised a hand this week. Finish the week first.

## Worked example

Pine Curve Lawn and Landscape, Gilmer. Fictional. Dale pasted his offer page into the exact working prompt in ChatGPT on a Sunday night.

Example output:

```text
Day 1: Text the three named boards and two referrals to ask for a 20-minute property walk this week. Post the page once to your Google Business Profile.
Days 2 to 5: Walk properties. Ask the five questions in order. Record answers the same day.
Day 6: Send each person a one-page summary and the sample report. Record replies.
Day 7: Score the week and write the decision.
Pass thresholds: 3 of 5 describe the missed-visit problem unprompted; 2 of 5 request a proposal or agreement; no price rejection without a stated reason.
Decision: continue to the first three founding properties, revise one element, or stop and revisit the buyer.
```

Review against the pass standard. Fixed window: seven days, Sunday to Sunday. Measurable signals: three thresholds with numbers. Consent-respecting outreach: three boards he already serves or who requested quotes, two warm introductions with the introducer's permission, and his own Google Business Profile. Clear decision: continue, revise, or stop, defined before the first walk.

What happened: five walks in six days. Four described the problem unprompted, and three of them used the word "chase." Two asked for the agreement that week. One said $1,250 was above what the board had budgeted and asked whether a twice-monthly winter schedule could bring it down. New objection, straight into the bank. The office park manager on 271 asked whether he could handle the parking lot islands, which were not on the scope card.

The one revision: he added a winter schedule option to the price sheet, quoted separately, and left the summer scope alone. Decision, written on the scorecard Sunday night: continue. Open the founding price to the first three properties. Two signed by the following Friday.

He never ran an ad. He ran five conversations, wrote them down, and decided.

## Exact working prompt

```text
Create a seven-day validation plan for [offer]. Use only permissioned outreach and owned channels. Define daily actions, conversation questions, pass/fail thresholds, what to record, and the decision at the end.
```

## Pass standard

The test has a fixed window, measurable signals, consent-respecting outreach, and a clear continue, revise, or stop decision.

## Common mistakes

- Going wide with cold outreach to feel productive. Fix: five people who know you or asked to hear from you.
- Setting the pass line after the week is over. Fix: thresholds on the scorecard before the first call.
- Counting compliments as signals. Fix: a booking, a signed agreement, or a specific question is a signal.
- Changing three things at once after the test. Fix: revise one element and run five more.
- Running ads to an untested page. Fix: five humans first, then The Local Ads Operator.
