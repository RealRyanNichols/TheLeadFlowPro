# LC L05: Build the Thank-You Path

## What you will finish

Turn form completion into delivery, orientation, and one next action. When you are done you will have a published or prototyped thank-you page that hands over the lead magnet, tells the buyer what to do first, and shows exactly one next step, tested on your own phone.

## Why this matters

The thank-you page is the most read page you will ever publish, and most businesses leave it as "Thanks! We'll be in touch." The buyer just did what you asked. They are paying more attention right now than they ever will again. And you hand them a blank wall.

Here is the scene. A lawn company in Gilmer had a solid fall prep checklist. The form worked. The thank-you page said "Success." The PDF went out by email, sometimes into spam. A third of the people who signed up never opened the checklist, and none of them knew there was a free yard walk-through on the table, because the page never said so.

Delivery is the promise you made in lesson four. "Enter your email and we will send it." If the thing does not show up in the next minute, the buyer decides you are like every other business that says one thing and does another. The thank-you page is where you keep the promise in front of their face while the email is still on its way.

This sits after consent because the page is the first thing the buyer sees after they agree, or do not agree, to hear from you. It has to respect that choice. If they left the boxes empty, the page still delivers everything, and the one next step is an invitation, not a chase.

## The lesson

### 1. Confirm success

The first line the buyer sees after clicking submit has to answer one question: did that work? Say it plainly. "Done. Your Fall Lawn Prep Checklist is on its way to sam@example.com."

Show the email address they typed. That does two things. It confirms the submission went through, and it lets them spot a typo before they spend ten minutes hunting a download that went to the wrong inbox. If your form tool cannot show the address, at least say "check the inbox you just entered."

Then tell them what to expect and when. "It should arrive within a minute. If it does not, check your spam or promotions folder and drag it to your inbox." That one line cuts your "I never got it" messages way down. Add a way to fix it: a resend button, or a plain line that says "Email us at hello@ and we will send it by hand."

What good looks like: a buyer who was unsure now knows it worked, where to look, and what to do if it is not there. The mistake people make: a bare "Thank you" with no mention of what was sent or where.

Example: the lawn company's page now reads "Done. The checklist is headed to the address you entered. Give it a minute, check spam if you have to, and here is a copy right now in case you are in a hurry."

### 2. Deliver immediately

Do not make them wait for the email. Put the lead magnet on the page. A download button for the PDF. A link to the calculator. The diagnostic embedded right there. The email is the backup. The page is the delivery.

Then orient them. What do I do first? What result should I expect? Write three lines. "Print it or open it on your phone. Walk the yard with it this weekend, front to back. You will end with a score and three things to do before the first freeze."

Keep the page short. Delivery, three lines of orientation, one troubleshooting line, one next step. If it scrolls more than one screen on a phone, cut something.

Two practical notes. If your form tool sends the email, make sure the sender is your domain, not the tool's. On the sites I build, Resend sends it with the domain verified, so the email comes from the business and lands in the inbox. And send the delivery email even if they left every consent box empty. That email is the transaction. It is what they asked for.

If you do not have a site that can hold a page like this, the free website build at /free-build is where I would start. Until then, a Notion page works as the prototype.

What good looks like: the buyer has the thing in hand within ten seconds of submitting. The mistake people make: hiding the lead magnet behind the email to "make sure the address is real." You already have the address. Deliver.

Example: a Longview bookkeeping coach put the quarterly checklist as a button on the thank-you page and moved the "what to do first" lines under it. The "where is my checklist" emails stopped that week.

### 3. Offer one logical next step

One. Not a menu. Not four buttons. One next step that follows from what they just got, in the branch where it makes sense.

The lead magnet ends with a decision, from lesson two. The thank-you page previews that decision. "When you finish the checklist, if your score is under 40, the free yard walk-through is the next thing to do. Book it here." That is a next step into the offer from the Offer Engine, said once, in plain words.

If they did not check a marketing box, the next step still shows. It is on a page they chose to visit. What it cannot do is promise a text or a call they did not agree to. "We will call you Tuesday" is off limits if they left the call box empty. "Book a time if you want one" is fine.

What good looks like: one button, one sentence about why, and it sits under the delivery, not above it. The mistake people make: turning the thank-you page into the homepage. Three offers, a newsletter box, a social bar. The buyer just gave you something. Give them the thing back, then show one door.

Example: Ledger and Lantern Bookkeeping in Longview (example) had four buttons on the thank-you page. They cut it to one, "Book a 20-minute books review," and put it under the checklist download.

Now run the prompt with your lead magnet and your offer filled in. You get confirmation copy, access instructions, first step, expected result, troubleshooting, and one next step. Trim it to one phone screen. Build it in your site editor or, if you are prototyping, a Notion page with the same order. Then open it on your phone, on cellular, not Wi-Fi, and read it out loud.

## Worked example

Sabine Lawn and Land in Gilmer is a fictional example. Two mowing crews and one owner who does the quotes. They give away the "Fall Lawn Prep Checklist," a one-page PDF with a score at the bottom. The paid offer is a seasonal lawn program, and the free yard walk-through is the door into it. Their old thank-you page said "Success! Check your email."

The owner ran the lesson prompt with the brackets filled in:

"Write a thank-you page for the Fall Lawn Prep Checklist. Include confirmation, access instructions, what to do first, what result to expect, troubleshooting, and one next step into the free yard walk-through that leads into the seasonal program. Keep it direct and useful."

Example output:

```text
Headline: Done. Your Fall Lawn Prep Checklist is on its way.
Confirmation: We sent it to the address you entered. Give it a minute and check spam if it is not there.
Access: Or open it right now: [Download the checklist]
What to do first: Walk the yard front to back with the checklist this weekend. Score each section.
What to expect: You will end with a score out of 60 and the three tasks that matter most before the first freeze.
Troubleshooting: Did not get the email? Email hello@ and we will send it by hand.
Next step: If your score comes in under 40, book the free yard walk-through. [Book the walk-through]
```

Review against the pass standard. Does the user receive the promised asset immediately? Yes, the download button is on the page, and the email is the backup. Exactly one primary next action? Yes, one button, tied to the score.

The one revision that made it better: on the owner's phone, the download button sat below the fold under the orientation lines. She moved it up, directly under the headline. Delivery first, then the reading, then the one door.

## Exact working prompt

```text
Write a thank-you page for [lead magnet]. Include confirmation, access instructions, what to do first, what result to expect, troubleshooting, and one next step into [offer]. Keep it direct and useful.
```

## Guided practice

1. Run the exact prompt with your lead magnet and your offer filled in.
2. Cut the result to one phone screen: confirmation, delivery, three orientation lines, one troubleshooting line, one next step.
3. Put the lead magnet itself on the page as a button or embed, and set the delivery email to send from your own domain.
4. Build the page in your site editor or a Notion prototype, submit your own form, and land on it.
5. Open it on your phone on cellular, read it out loud, and move the download above anything it sits under.

## Assignment and evidence

Publish or prototype the thank-you page and test it on mobile.

Save the source material, the prompt, the first result, the final result, and your review notes.

## Pass standard

The user receives the promised asset immediately and sees exactly one primary next action.

## Common mistakes

- "Thanks, check your email" and nothing else. Fix: put the lead magnet on the page and confirm where the email went.
- Delivery only by email. Fix: the page delivers, the email backs it up.
- Four next steps. Fix: one button, tied to the decision the lead magnet ends with.
- Promising a call or text the buyer did not agree to. Fix: offer a booking link instead of a promise to reach out.
- Never opening it on a phone. Fix: submit your own form on cellular and read the page out loud.
