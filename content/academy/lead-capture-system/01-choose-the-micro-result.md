# LC L01: Choose the Micro Result

## What you will finish

Give away a small result that naturally points to the paid next step. When you are done you will have one lead magnet idea picked out of ten, with a one-sentence promise you could say to a customer at the counter without reading it.

## Why this matters

Most free downloads are a brochure with a title on it. The owner spent a weekend on it, nobody finishes it, and the only thing it produces is an email address that goes cold.

Here is the scene. A roofer in Marshall puts "Download our free roofing guide" on his site. Twelve pages. Company history, warranty language, a photo of the truck. Forty people download it in a year. He calls none of them, because the guide told him nothing about who has a leaking roof right now.

A micro result is different. It is one small job the buyer can finish in twenty minutes that leaves something in their hands. The right one also shows the buyer, on their own, when they need what you sell. That is the whole trick. The free thing is the first step of the paid thing.

This lesson comes first because every other lesson in this course builds on the choice you make here. The form, the consent wording, the thank-you page, the qualification questions, the lead record. All of it exists to deliver this one result and route the person who wanted it.

## The lesson

### 1. Start with one urgent question

Bring the buyer brief from the Offer Engine (OE L01) and the offer page from OE L07. If you skipped that course, write one buyer and one paid offer on a sticky note before you go further. One buyer. One offer. Not three.

Now ask: what is the question this buyer is already asking themselves the week before they call me?

Not the question you wish they asked. The one they actually type into Google at 9 pm. For a plumber it is "how old is my water heater and is it about to go." For a dental assistant school it is "can I do this program while I keep my job." For a lawn company it is "why is my yard brown in patches when the neighbor's is green."

Write five of those questions. Pick the one that is most urgent and most tied to your offer. Urgent means they will spend twenty minutes on it today. Tied to your offer means the answer to the question sometimes turns out to be "call a professional."

What good looks like: the question makes the buyer nod. The mistake people make: they pick the question the owner finds interesting instead of the one the buyer finds urgent. Nobody is urgent about your company history.

Example: a gym owner in Kilgore wants to give away "our training philosophy." The urgent question in his buyer's head is "I have not worked out in two years, what do I do the first week so I do not quit again." That question is the lead magnet.

### 2. Make the result finishable

A micro result has three properties. It takes under twenty minutes. It produces something tangible the buyer can look at when done. And it ends. There is a last step.

Tangible means a filled-in sheet, a number, a score, a short list, a decision. "Learn about X" is not tangible. "Find the age of your water heater from the serial number and score it against five warning signs" is tangible. The buyer ends with a number and a list.

Here is the test I use. Can the buyer do this standing in their kitchen with their phone, without calling me, without buying anything, and without reading anything longer than a page? If the answer is no, cut it down until the answer is yes.

That is how our missed call calculator at /tools/missed-call-calculator works for us. You type in a few numbers, you get a number back, and the number tells you whether you have a problem worth fixing. Two minutes. Something in your hands.

What good looks like: the buyer can tell a friend what they got. "I did this thing and my water heater is eleven years old and hitting three of the five signs." The mistake people make: they give away a course when they should give away a checklist. Bigger is not more generous. Bigger is unfinished.

Example: a Longview realtor wants to give away a forty-page home selling guide. Cut it to a twenty-minute pre-listing walkthrough. Twelve rooms, three questions each, a total score at the bottom. Finishable.

### 3. Connect it to the core offer

This is the bridge. The free result has to point at the paid offer without a sales pitch. The best way to do that is to make the free result reveal a condition, and make the paid offer the thing you do when that condition is true.

Water heater scores three or more warning signs: that is when a replacement quote makes sense. Pre-listing walkthrough scores under 60: that is when a listing consult makes sense. Two-week restart plan finished: that is when a coached membership makes sense.

Write the bridge as one sentence: "If your [result] shows [condition], the next step is [paid offer]." If you cannot write that sentence, the idea is not connected. Pick a different one.

One plain rule here. The free result has to be honestly useful when the condition is false too. If the checklist is rigged so everyone "needs" a replacement, people can smell it, and you burn trust in your own town. Useful on its own, connected when it should be.

What good looks like: same buyer, same problem, same words on the free thing and the paid thing. The mistake: the lead magnet is for a broad audience and the paid offer is for a narrow one, so the list fills with people who will never buy. Match them.

Now run the prompt. Fill in [buyer] from your brief and [result] with the outcome your offer produces. You get ten ideas ranked by usefulness, speed, and buyer fit. Read the top three out loud. Pick the one you could explain to a customer at the counter in one breath.

## Worked example

Caddo Lake Plumbing in Marshall is a fictional example. Two trucks, owner on the jobs most days. They sell whole-home plumbing inspections and water heater replacements. The buyer brief from the Offer Engine says: homeowner, house built before 2010, no plumber they trust, hates surprise bills.

The owner ran the lesson prompt with the brackets filled in:

"Generate ten lead magnet ideas for a homeowner in East Texas with a house over fifteen years old who wants to avoid a surprise plumbing failure. Each must be completed in under twenty minutes, create a tangible output, and naturally reveal when the paid offer is useful. Rank them by usefulness, speed, and buyer fit."

Example output:

```text
1. Water heater age and risk check: decode the serial number, score five warning signs. 10 minutes. Reveals replacement need. Fit: high.
2. Shutoff valve locator: find and label every shutoff in the house. 15 minutes. Reveals stuck valves. Fit: high.
3. Slab leak early warning sheet: five signs and a meter test. 15 minutes. Fit: medium.
4. Monthly water bill baseline worksheet. 20 minutes. Fit: medium.
5. Kitchen and bath fixture inventory. 20 minutes. Fit: low, no clear paid step.
```

Review against the pass standard. Idea one is useful on its own: even if the heater is fine, the homeowner now knows its age and has a date to recheck. It is connected to the same buyer and the same problem as the paid inspection. Idea five fails: a fixture inventory is busywork, and it never reveals when Caddo Lake is useful.

The one revision that made it better: the first draft of the promise read "Learn how to check your water heater." The owner rewrote it as "Find your water heater's age and its risk score in ten minutes, using the label on the tank." Specific, finishable, and the score is the bridge to the paid inspection.

## Exact working prompt

```text
Generate ten lead magnet ideas for [buyer] who wants [result]. Each must be completed in under twenty minutes, create a tangible output, and naturally reveal when the paid offer is useful. Rank them by usefulness, speed, and buyer fit.
```

## Guided practice

1. Write the one buyer and one paid offer from the Offer Engine at the top of a blank page.
2. List five questions that buyer is already asking the week before they call you, and circle the most urgent one.
3. Run the exact prompt with your [buyer] and [result] filled in.
4. Cross out every idea that takes over twenty minutes or ends without something tangible.
5. For the top three, write the bridge sentence: "If your [result] shows [condition], the next step is [paid offer]."

## Assignment and evidence

Select one lead magnet and write its one-sentence promise.

Save the source material, the prompt, the first result, the final result, and your review notes.

## Pass standard

The free result is useful on its own and connected to the same buyer and problem as the paid offer.

## Common mistakes

- Picking the topic you find interesting. Fix: pick the question the buyer types into Google at 9 pm.
- Giving away something too big to finish. Fix: cut it until it fits in twenty minutes and has a last step.
- A free result with no bridge. Fix: write the "if it shows X, the next step is Y" sentence or drop the idea.
- Rigging the result so everyone "needs" the paid offer. Fix: make it honestly useful when the answer is no.
- Choosing a broad lead magnet for a narrow offer. Fix: same buyer, same problem, same words on both.
