# FU L04: Handle No Response

## What you will finish

Use respectful check-ins, channel changes, and a clean close-the-loop message. You will have three short messages, a gentle reminder, a useful alternative, and a close-the-loop note, each under 90 words, each with an easy way to say not now, slotted into your sequence with stop rules.

## Why this matters

Silence is the most common reply you will ever get. Not no. Not yes. Nothing. And most business owners handle silence one of two bad ways. They quit after one try, because silence feels like rejection. Or they send the same message again, harder, because silence feels like an insult.

Silence is usually neither. The homeowner got your quote on Tuesday, the water heater is still limping along, and her mother went into the hospital Wednesday. You are not on her list. You are not off her list either. You are just not on the list.

Here is a scene from a plumber in Longview. The quote for a water heater replacement went out by email. Five days, nothing. The owner is annoyed. He sends "Did you get my quote??" Two question marks. She did get it. Now she feels scolded and picks the other guy. A gentle reminder and a useful alternative would have kept the door open. A close-the-loop note would have ended it with respect, and often enough the close-the-loop note is the one that gets the reply.

You built the seven-touch sequence in lesson three. This lesson gives you the three messages that go in the gaps when a touch gets nothing back, and the rule for when to stop.

## The lesson

### 1. Assume distraction, not rejection

Write every no-response message from the assumption that they are busy, not that they are ignoring you. That one assumption changes every word.

"Did you get my quote?" assumes they owe you an answer. "Bringing this back to the top of your inbox in case it got buried" assumes they are human. Same information, different posture.

Practically, a gentle reminder does three things. It names the business and the request again, because it has been days and your first response is gone from their memory. It restates the useful thing in one line: the range, the day, the link. And it asks a question they can answer in one word.

The channel question matters here. If two emails got nothing, the third email probably will not either. Change the channel. A short human call from Quo at a reasonable hour. A text, if they opted in, sent by a person. Same message, different door. If they never consented to anything but email, stay on email and space it out.

Timing: a gentle reminder goes out two to four days after the touch that got no answer. Not the next morning.

What good looks like: if they read the reminder and did nothing, they would still think well of you. The mistake: guilt. "I have not heard back from you." "I sent this twice." Guilt gets a reply from almost nobody and an unsubscribe from a few.

Example: "Hi Tom, Jake at Gregg County Plumbing. Resending the water heater quote from Tuesday in case it got buried. Still $1,850 installed, still able to do it this week. Want me to hold Thursday, or is next week better? If the timing is off, just say so." Example price.

### 2. Offer an easy answer

The second no-response message stops asking for the decision and offers something instead. A useful alternative. It is the touch that says "I am still helping, even if you are not buying."

The alternative depends on the trigger. For a quote: a smaller option, a payment plan if you have one, or a "what to check in the meantime" note. For a download: the next piece they will need. For a missed call: "here is the answer to the question most people call about, in case that was yours." For a booking they never confirmed: two new time options.

Then the easy answer. Give them words to decline. "Reply not now and I will check back in the spring." "If you went with someone else, no hard feelings, tell me and I will close this out." Making it easy to say no gets you more honest yeses, and it gets you the no faster, which frees your time for the people who are ready.

Keep it under 90 words. That is the prompt's rule and it is a good one. Under 90 words fits on a phone screen without scrolling. Count them.

What good looks like: the person could use your alternative without buying anything from you, and you would be fine with that. The mistake: the "alternative" is a discount. Discounts train people to go silent and wait. Offer value or a smaller step, not a lower price.

Example: "Tom, one more thought on the water heater. If replacement is not in the budget this month, I can do a flush and anode check for $140 that usually buys some time. Want that instead, or should I check back in October? Either answer is fine." Example price.

### 3. Close the loop

The last message ends the sequence out loud. It says: I am going to stop now, here is how to reach me later, thank you.

This is not a trick. It is the polite thing to do, and it also works, because it is the first message that asks for nothing. "I will close this out on my end" gives the person permission to reply without committing to anything. Plenty of people reply to this one with "sorry, crazy week, yes, Thursday works."

Three parts. Name the business and the request one last time. State that you are closing it and will not keep messaging. Leave one door open: "reply anytime" or "here is my direct number."

Then the stop rules, and this is where you tie into the record. When the close-the-loop message sends, the status changes to "closed, no response," the next action clears, and the sequence is finished. No more automated sends. If they reply later, the reply reopens the record and a human takes it from there.

Write the stop rules for all three messages into your sequence planner. Gentle reminder: stop if reply, booking, purchase, or opt-out. Useful alternative: same. Close the loop: the sequence ends regardless.

What good looks like: someone who never replied still has your number and a good impression. The mistake: a fake close. "This is my last email" followed by three more. If you say you are closing it, close it.

Example: "Tom, Jake at Gregg County Plumbing. I will close out the water heater quote on my end so I stop filling your inbox. If it comes back up, my direct line is 903 555 0122 and the quote holds for 30 days. Thanks for considering us."

## Worked example

Gregg County Plumbing in Longview is a fictional example. Three trucks, and the owner runs dispatch from his phone. Context: a water heater quote emailed Tuesday, one first response, one sequence touch, no reply after six days. Email consent yes, text consent no, phone number on file.

The owner ran the prompt with the context filled in:

"Write three no-response messages for a water heater replacement quote sent by email six days ago with no reply, homeowner in Longview, email only: a gentle reminder, a useful alternative, and a close-the-loop note. Each must be under 90 words and include an easy way to say not now."

Example output (gentle reminder):

```text
Subject: Water heater quote from Tuesday
Hi Tom, Jake with Gregg County Plumbing. Bringing Tuesday's quote back to the top in case it got buried.
Still $1,850 installed, permit included, and I can still do it this week.
Would Thursday work, or is next week better?
If now is not the time, reply "not now" and I will check back in a month.
```

Review against the pass standard. Contextual: yes, it names the quote and the day. Low pressure: yes. Easy to decline: yes, "not now" is spelled out. The close-the-loop draft had a problem. It ended with "Last chance to lock in this price before it goes up." That is pressure disguised as a goodbye, and the price was not going up.

The one revision: the close-the-loop note became "I will close this on my end so I stop filling your inbox. The quote holds for 30 days if it comes back up. My direct line is 903 555 0122." No threat, well under 90 words. Since Tom never opted in to texts, the owner also removed the "I will shoot you a text" line the model had put in the alternative, and kept all three on email with a call as the only channel change.

## Exact working prompt

```text
Write three no-response messages for [context]: a gentle reminder, a useful alternative, and a close-the-loop note. Each must be under 90 words and include an easy way to say not now.
```

## Guided practice

4. Add the three messages to your sequence planner as the responses to silence after touches two, four, and seven, each with its stop rule.
5. Send the gentle reminder to that real lead today, by the channel they agreed to.

## Pass standard

Messages remain contextual, low pressure, and easy to decline.

## Common mistakes

- "Did you get my email?" Fix: restate the useful thing and ask a one-word question.
- Silence on email answered with more email. Fix: change the channel if they consented to another one, otherwise space it out.
- The alternative is a discount. Fix: offer a smaller step or a useful note, not a lower price.
- A fake last message. Fix: when you close the loop, the record closes and the sends stop.
- No way to say no. Fix: write the words "not now" into the message.
