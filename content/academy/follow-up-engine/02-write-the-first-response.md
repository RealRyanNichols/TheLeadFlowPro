# FU L02: Write the First Response

## What you will finish

Confirm the request and make the next step easy. You will leave with three versions of your first response for one trigger, an email, a text, and a voicemail, each short enough to read aloud in under thirty seconds, saved in the First-response builder.

## Why this matters

The first response is the one message almost everyone reads. Later touches get skimmed. This one gets opened because they just asked you for something and they are waiting for it.

And most first responses waste it. "Thank you for contacting us. A member of our team will be in touch shortly." No business name in the body. No mention of what they asked for. No next step. The person reads it, cannot tell if it is real, and goes back to searching.

Picture a detailer in Marshall. A customer fills out the ceramic coating quote form at 9 pm from the couch. The auto-reply says "We got your message!" That is it. The next morning she has three quotes from three other shops that actually named a price range and a day. The Marshall shop never had a chance.

You built the trigger map in lesson one. Now you write the message each trigger deserves. Lesson three turns this single message into a sequence, so get this one right first.

## The lesson

### 1. Lead with context

Start with who you are and what they did. In that order, in the first line.

"Hi Dana, this is Marcus at Caddo Lake Detailing. You asked for a ceramic coating quote on a 2021 F-150 last night." That is the whole job of the first sentence. Business name. Their request, in specific terms. The specific terms matter. "Your quote request" is weak. "Ceramic coating on a 2021 F-150" proves a person read it.

Pull those details straight from the lead record. If your form captured vehicle, service, and preferred day (that is the qualification work from LC L06), use every one of them. A field you collected and never mention was not worth collecting.

What good looks like: they can identify the business and remember the request from the first line alone, on a phone lock screen, without opening the message. The mistake: opening with "Thank you for reaching out." It is polite and empty. Nobody knows who "us" is.

Example, download trigger. Weak: "Thanks for downloading!" Strong: "Hi Karen, this is Lisa at Rose City Fitness. Here is the First Two Weeks plan you asked for." The strong one works even if she never reads line two.

### 2. Answer the obvious question

Every trigger comes with a question the person is already asking in their head. Answer it before they have to ask.

Quote request: "What does this roughly cost and when can you come?" Give a range and a window. "Coatings on a truck that size run $800 to $1,400 depending on paint condition. I have Thursday and Saturday morning open." Example numbers, yours will differ. Notice this is not the final price. It is enough to keep them in the conversation.

Download: "Where is the thing I asked for?" Put the link in the first two lines. Deliver first, talk second.

Missed call: "Who is this and why are you calling me?" Name the business and say you are returning their call.

Purchase: "Did it go through, and what happens now?" Confirm the payment and state the next step with a date.

Quote sent: "Is this the whole price, and what do I do to say yes?" Confirm what is included and how to accept.

What good looks like: the person does not have to reply just to get basic information. The mistake: hiding the answer to force a call. People do not chase you. They chase the competitor who answered.

One honesty rule. If you do not know the answer yet, say when you will. "I need to see the paint in person to quote it exactly. I can do that Thursday." That is an answer.

### 3. Ask for one action

End with one thing to do. One. Not "call us, email us, or visit our website."

The action should be the smallest step that moves the conversation. Pick a day. Reply with a photo. Confirm the address. Say yes to the quote. Make it answerable in one word or one tap.

Match the size of the ask to the trigger. A download gets a tiny ask, "which of these is your goal?" A quote request can carry a bigger one, "which morning works?" A purchase gets a confirmation, not a sales question at all.

Now write it three ways, because each channel has different rules.

Email: the subject line names the request. Three to six short lines. A link if you are delivering something. A signature with your name, the business, and your phone number. Send it from your own domain through Resend, not a free address, so it lands in the inbox instead of the promotions tab.

Text: under 300 characters if you can. Business name first, their request second, one question third. Sent by a human from Quo, only to people who opted in to texts, never before 8 am or after 8 pm. If they did not opt in, there is no text version for this trigger. Skip it and let email and the phone do the work.

Voicemail: twenty seconds. Your name, the business, what they asked for, the one thing you need, and your number twice, slowly. Record yourself and listen back. If you sound like you are reading, redo it.

What good looks like: all three say the same thing at different lengths. The mistake: adding urgency you made up. "Only two spots left this week" when you have six is a lie, and the customer finds out. The prompt says no false urgency for a reason. Real scarcity is fine. Say Thursday is your last open morning if it is.

Read every version out loud. If you stumble, the customer will too.

## Worked example

Caddo Lake Detailing in Marshall is a fictional example. One bay, the owner plus one tech. Trigger: ceramic coating quote form. The lead record shows vehicle, service, preferred day, email consent yes, text consent yes.

The owner ran the lesson prompt with the trigger and business filled in:

"Write the first follow-up for a ceramic coating quote form from Caddo Lake Detailing. Mention exactly what the person requested, deliver or confirm it, answer the likely next question, and ask for one small next action. Give email, SMS, and voicemail versions. Do not add false urgency."

Example output (SMS version):

```text
Hi Dana, Marcus at Caddo Lake Detailing. Got your ceramic coating request for the 2021 F-150.
Trucks that size usually run $800 to $1,400 depending on paint condition.
I have Thursday and Saturday mornings open to look at it and give you an exact number.
Which works better?
Reply STOP to opt out.
```

Review against the pass standard. Identify the business: yes, first line. Remember the request: yes, coating and truck both named. Act without decoding: yes, one question with a one-word answer. The email version passed the same way. The voicemail version failed. It ran forty seconds and said the phone number once, fast, at the very end.

The one revision: the voicemail became "Hi Dana, Marcus at Caddo Lake Detailing, returning your ceramic coating request on the F-150. I can look at it Thursday or Saturday morning. Call or text me at 903 555 0148. Again, 903 555 0148." Nineteen seconds. Number twice. Done.

## Exact working prompt

```text
Write the first follow-up for [trigger] from [business]. Mention exactly what the person requested, deliver or confirm it, answer the likely next question, and ask for one small next action. Give email, SMS, and voicemail versions. Do not add false urgency.
```

## Guided practice

1. Pick the trigger from your lesson one map that fires most often.
2. Fill in the prompt with that trigger and your business name, then run it.
3. Check the first line of each version for the business name and the specific request.
4. Cut until the email is six lines, the text is under 300 characters, and the voicemail is twenty seconds.
5. Read all three aloud and record the voicemail on your phone.

## Assignment and evidence

Write and read aloud all three channel versions.

Save the source material, the prompt, the first result, the final result, and your review notes.

## Pass standard

The recipient can identify the business, remember the request, and act without decoding the message.

## Common mistakes

- "Thanks for reaching out" with no business name. Fix: business name and their request in line one.
- Hiding the price range to force a call. Fix: give the range and the window, then ask for the day.
- Three calls to action. Fix: one question with a one-word answer.
- Invented urgency. Fix: only say a slot is scarce when it is.
- A text version for someone who never opted in. Fix: no consent, no text. Email and call instead.
