# Google Ads for a Longview service business: tracking first, then spend

Google Ads is the fastest way to be at the top of the page when someone in Longview types "water heater repair" or "roof leak" at nine at night. It is also the fastest way to spend a thousand dollars on clicks from three counties away for a service you do not offer.

The difference between those two outcomes is set up before the first campaign runs. Here is the order, and why it matters for a local service business.

## First: your own account

- Create the Google Ads account under a Google account your business controls, with your billing profile on it. Ad spend goes from you to Google, never through a manager.
- If an agency already runs ads for you, ask for manager access to be granted to you, not the other way around. If the account is not yours, the history, the conversion data, and the learning that makes ads cheaper over time leave when the agency does.
- Connect the account to your Google Business Profile, so ads can show your location, hours, and reviews.

## Second: tracking, or you are guessing

For a service business the two conversions that matter are calls and forms. Set both up before spending.

- **Calls.** Google's call conversion tracking counts calls from the ad and from your website. If you use a tracking number, it should be yours and it should forward to your real line.
- **Forms.** The Google Tag on your site records a form submission as a conversion. Test it with a real submission.
- **Booked appointments**, if you take bookings online, are the best conversion of all because they are closer to money.

Then check one thing every month: does a conversion in Google Ads match a lead record in your own inbox or CRM? If Google says twelve and your records say four, the tracking is wrong, and every decision built on it is wrong too.

## Third: keywords that match the job

Local service ads waste money in three predictable ways.

1. **Broad match on a broad word.** "Plumber" buys clicks for plumber jobs, plumber salaries, and plumber jokes. Start with the exact services you want more of: "tankless water heater install Longview."
2. **No negative keywords.** Add the words you never want to pay for: free, DIY, jobs, salary, school, the towns you do not serve. Review the search-terms report every week for the first month.
3. **The wrong radius.** Draw the service area you actually drive to. A click from Shreveport is not a lead if you stop at the state line.

## Fourth: a page that matches the search

Send "roof leak repair" to a roof leak page, not the home page. The page should say the service, the area, the price or how pricing works, real proof, and one big call button. On a phone, the call button is the whole page.

## Fifth: the report that tells you what to do next

Every week, four numbers and one decision:

- Spend, paid to Google.
- Lead records by source (search campaign, local campaign, and everything else you run).
- Cost per lead record, which is spend divided by records. It compares campaigns. It is not profit.
- What happened next: how many became quotes, how many became jobs, from your own records.

Then one decision: raise, hold, or cut, and which keyword or page to fix. That is the whole report. A dashboard with forty charts and no decision is decoration.

## What Google Ads cannot do

It cannot promise a position, a number of calls, or a return. It cannot fix a phone that goes to voicemail. It cannot make a page with no prices convert. Anyone offering guaranteed leads is selling you the guarantee, not the leads.
