# Facebook and Instagram ads for East Texas businesses: what to set up before you spend a dollar

The most common Meta ads story in East Texas goes like this. An owner boosts a post, gets a handful of messages, replies to some of them two days later, and concludes that Facebook does not work for their business. Nothing about that story is about the ad. It is about what was not set up before the ad ran.

This is the setup. Do it once, in your own name, and every campaign after it gets easier to judge.

## 1. The accounts, in your name

Before creative, before budget:

- **A Meta Business Manager** owned by your business, not by a freelancer or an agency.
- **An ad account** inside it, with your card on it. Ad spend goes from you to Meta. It should never pass through a third party.
- **Your Facebook Page and Instagram account** connected to that Business Manager.
- **Access for whoever helps you** granted as a partner, which you can revoke in one click.

If someone else already runs ads for you, the first job is moving the account, the pixel, and the audiences into your Business Manager. Everything built on them is your asset.

## 2. The pixel and the events

The pixel is the small piece of code that tells Meta what happened after the click. Without it you are paying for clicks and guessing about leads.

- Install the pixel on your website, or connect it through your site platform.
- Set up the events that matter: a lead (form submitted), a contact (call or text tapped), and if you sell online, a purchase.
- If your form posts to a server, connect the Conversions API as well, so a lead still counts when a browser blocks the pixel.
- Test it with one real submission before any ad runs.

## 3. The lead route

Every lead needs a home and an owner within minutes, not days.

- Decide where a lead lands: your inbox, your CRM, or a simple record with the person's name, what they asked, and the source.
- Make sure the person who answers the phone gets an alert.
- Write the first reply in advance, so answering takes one tap.
- If you use Meta's instant forms, connect them to the same route. A lead sitting inside Ads Manager is a lead nobody will call.

Speed matters more than the ad. The business that answers first usually gets the job.

## 4. Consent, before any text goes out

If you plan to text leads, the form has to say so and the person has to agree. Meta's instant forms can carry a consent question. Your website form should have a checkbox. Keep the record. Honor STOP immediately. A great ad that produces texts nobody agreed to is a liability, not a campaign.

## 5. One offer, one page

Run one offer at a time, and send the click to a page built for that offer: what it is, what it costs or how pricing works, proof that is real, and one form. A home page with six services is where ad clicks go to die.

## 6. The trace, from ad to outcome

This is the part most East Texas businesses skip, and the part that makes the next decision easy. Every lead record should carry the source (which ad, which form), the action (called, texted, submitted), and the outcome (booked, quoted, won, lost). After thirty days you can look at a list, not a feeling.

Two definitions worth writing on the wall:

- A **lead record** is an inquiry, not a customer. Two records can be the same person.
- **Cost per lead record** is spend divided by records. It is useful for comparing ads. It is not revenue.

Nobody can honestly promise you a cost per lead or a return on ad spend before the data exists. Anyone who does is guessing with your money.

## 7. Budget: the honest question

How much are you genuinely prepared to spend per month, paid directly to Meta, for at least sixty days? A small, steady number beats a big number for two weeks. And a zero right now is a fine answer; it just means the first job is the website and the follow-up, not the ads.
